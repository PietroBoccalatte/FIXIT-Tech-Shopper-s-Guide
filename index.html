Why this matters

Electronic waste makes up a small share of all waste by volume, but it contains a disproportionate share of the hazardous material. Fast-moving electronics have short lifespans and are hard to recycle.

What to look for: Devices with long lifespans, replaceable parts and a build that is made to last.

With so many devices to choose from, it can be hard to know which are more sustainable. In honour of Tech Week, the Sevenoaks Fixit team have made this shopping guide for you.

What we looked for

  • ♻️ Repairability
    Widely available parts and repair manuals
  • 🔄 Refurbished options
    Tested used devices, often with warranties, for less money
  • ⚙️ Modularity
    Upgrade parts of a device instead of replacing the whole thing

🛠️ Maintenance & longevity tips

  • Keep it cool: Heat is the enemy of electronics. Use your device on surfaces that allow air circulation, avoid direct sunlight, and keep vents clear.
  • Battery care: Try to keep your battery between 20% and 80%. On newer devices you can turn on optimised battery charging to improve battery lifespan and performance.
  • Regular software updates: Keep your devices up to date to make sure they run smoothly and are protected from malware.
  • Use a protective case: Cases and screen protectors are cheap and easy to fit, so use them.
  • Clean dust filters: On laptops and desktops, dust build-up reduces cooling efficiency. Use compressed air quarterly on vents.
  • Use your right to repair: Learn how to replace a battery, screen or storage. iFixit and manufacturer guides make it accessible.
  • Buy refurbished parts: Batteries and charging cables sourced used or refurbished further reduce e-waste and cost.
